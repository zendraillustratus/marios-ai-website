01 — Foundations

What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence is the simulation of human intelligence by machines. It enables computers to think, learn, reason, and make decisions — tasks that previously required the human brain.

🧠
Learning
AI systems improve from experience. The more data they see, the better they become — without being explicitly programmed for every scenario.
🔄
Reasoning
From chess to drug discovery, AI uses logical chains to evaluate outcomes and find optimal solutions across millions of possibilities.
👁️
Perception
Computer vision, speech recognition, and sensory processing let AI understand the world the same way humans do — through sight and sound.
💬
Language
Natural Language Processing gives machines the ability to read, write, translate, and converse in ways that feel genuinely human.
02 — Origins

History of AI

The journey from academic concept to everyday reality — spanning seven decades of breakthroughs, winters, and revolutions.

1950
Alan Turing asks: Can machines think?
Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" — laying the philosophical foundation for AI.
1956
Dartmouth Conference — AI is born
John McCarthy coins the term "Artificial Intelligence." The field officially begins.
1966
ELIZA — The first chatbot
Joseph Weizenbaum creates ELIZA at MIT, the first program to simulate human conversation.
1986
Deep Learning revived
Geoffrey Hinton and others bring back neural networks, laying groundwork for modern deep learning.
1997
Deep Blue beats Kasparov
IBM's Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov — AI's first major media moment.
2012
AlexNet — The deep learning revolution
A convolutional neural network wins ImageNet by a landslide. The AI boom begins.
2020
GPT-3 — Language takes off
OpenAI's GPT-3 shows that scale + transformer architecture = remarkably fluent AI text generation.
2023–2026
Generative AI goes mainstream
ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Sora, and hundreds of AI tools transform how we work, create, and communicate.
03 — Classification

Types of AI

Narrow AI

AI that does one thing brilliantly

Also called Weak AI — this is what we have today. Spam filters, Siri, recommendation algorithms, and AlphaFold are all narrow AI. They outperform humans at their specific task but can't generalize beyond it.

✓ Already everywhere
✓ Highly reliable at its task
✓ Powers modern tech products

AGI

Artificial General Intelligence

The hypothetical AI that matches human intelligence across any domain — reasoning, creativity, social intelligence, and physical dexterity. No system has achieved this yet. It's the north star of AI research.

○ Would match human cognition
○ Self-aware and adaptable
○ Still a research goal
🤖
The long game.
Not yet achieved.

ASI

Artificial Super Intelligence

A hypothetical AI that surpasses all human intelligence combined. Beyond science fiction's "Skynet" — this is a serious philosophical debate in AI safety circles. Some believe it's decades away; others say it's impossible by definition.

04 — Technology

Key Technologies

The building blocks that make modern AI possible — from statistics to neural networks.

📊
Machine Learning
The backbone of modern AI. ML algorithms find patterns in data and use them to make predictions — from Netflix recommendations to fraud detection.
🧬
Deep Learning
Multi-layered neural networks that learn hierarchical representations. Powers image recognition, voice assistants, and generative AI.
💬
Natural Language Processing
The bridge between human language and machine understanding. Enables chatbots, translation, search, and text generation at near-human fluency.
👁️
Computer Vision
Teaching machines to see. From facial recognition to medical imaging to self-driving cars — CV gives AI eyes.
🔗
Transformers & LLMs
The architecture behind GPT, Claude, and Gemini. Attention mechanisms let AI process entire documents at once — revolutionizing language understanding.
🤖
Reinforcement Learning
AI learns through trial and error, optimizing for rewards. Used in game AI (AlphaGo), robotics, and training autonomous systems.
